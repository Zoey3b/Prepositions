Module 1 · Time Prepositions

When to Use AT, ON, and IN (Time)

Understand the three time prepositions with clear rules, examples, and memory tricks.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use at correctly with times of day and fixed points
  • Use on correctly with days and specific dates
  • Use in correctly with longer periods
  • Avoid the most common time preposition mistakes
The Big Three
Special Cases
On Time vs In Time
Quiz

AT Specific clock times & fixed points

at at 5 o'clock
at at midnight / at noon
at at lunchtime / at sunset
at at the weekend
at at Christmas / at Easter
at at the moment / at present
📌

Memory Trick: AT = A sharp Point on the clock

Think of AT as a needle pointing to an exact moment — like a clock hand pointing to exactly 3:00. A point in time, not a period.

ON Specific days & dates

on on Monday / on Mondays
on on 16 May 2012
on on New Year's Day
on on my birthday
on on Friday morning / on Sunday afternoon
📅

Memory Trick: ON = On a calendar page

Imagine placing your finger ON a date in a calendar. Days and dates are things you can point at on a page.

IN Longer periods: months, years, seasons

in in June / in October
in in 2012 / in the 1990s
in in winter / in summer
in in the morning / in the afternoon
in in the past / in the future
in in a few minutes / in three weeks
📦

Memory Trick: IN = Inside a container of time

Think of a month or year as a box you're inside. You live IN June the way you live in a room — surrounded by it.

⚡ Quick Recap

AT
Exact time / fixed points
ON
Days & specific dates
IN
Months / years / seasons

🌙 Special Cases to Know

ExpressionPrepositionExample
night (in general)atI don't like working at night.
a particular nightinI heard a noise in the night.
Christmas (period)atWe give presents at Christmas.
Christmas Day (specific)onI'm home on Christmas Day.
morning/afternoon/eveninginI'll see you in the morning.
Friday morning (specific)onI'll see you on Friday morning.
the moment / presentatI'm busy at the moment.
the same timeatWe arrived at the same time.

🚫 NO Preposition Needed

We do not use at/on/in before: last, next, this, every

❌ Common Mistakes

on next Friday
✓ next Friday
in last June
✓ last June
at every morning
✓ every morning

🕐 On Time vs In Time

ON time
= Punctual. Exactly when expected.
"The train left on time."
IN time
= Soon enough. Not too late.
"I got home in time for dinner."

❌ Common Mistake

I arrived in time (meaning: exactly at the scheduled time)
✓ I arrived on time

📌 At the End vs In the End

AT the end (of…)
= When something finishes.
"At the end of the film, everyone clapped."
IN the end
= Finally, after a long time.
"We sold the car in the end."
Module 2 · Position Prepositions

Where to Use AT, ON, and IN (Position)

Learn to describe where things are using the right preposition every time.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use in for spaces, enclosed areas, and bodies of water
  • Use at for points and specific locations
  • Use on for surfaces, floors, and sides
  • Handle tricky cases: in/at/on the corner, front/back
Basic Rules
Advanced Cases
Buildings & Places
Quiz

IN Enclosed spaces & bodies

in in a room / in a building / in a box
in in a garden / in the city centre
in in a pool / in the sea / in a river
in in Italy / in Venice (countries & cities)
in in the sky / in the world
in in a newspaper / in a book / in a photo
📦

Memory Trick: IN = you can be surrounded by it

If you can be surrounded by it (walls, water, sky), use IN. You're inside it, not just touching it.

AT Specific points & locations

at at the bus stop / at the door
at at the traffic lights / at the roundabout
at at her desk / at reception
at at the top / at the bottom / at the end
at at the corner (of a street)

ON Surfaces & sides

on on the floor / on the ceiling / on the wall
on on the table / on a chair
on on page 7 / on a map / on a website
on on the first floor / on the left / on the right
on on a river / on a road / on the coast
on on an island

🔀 Tricky Comparisons

SituationExample
In the shop (inside, crowded)"There were many people in the shop."
At the shop (point/direction)"Turn left at the shop."
In the bottle (water inside)"There is water in the bottle."
On the bottle (label on surface)"There is a label on the bottle."
At the door (somebody there)"There is somebody at the door."
On the door (notice on surface)"There is a notice on the door."

Module 3 · Movement & Transport

TO, INTO, BY and Transport Prepositions

Express movement and how you travel with the right prepositions.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use to for movement toward a place
  • Distinguish in vs on for transport types
  • Use by to describe how you travel
  • Use into and out of correctly
Going TO
Transport
Into & Arrive
Quiz

➡️ Movement: Going TO a Place

to go to China / travel to London
to go to work / go to school
to go to a party / come to my house
to drive to the airport / return to Italy
Compare TO (movement) vs IN/AT (position):
They are going to France. ↔ They live in France.
Can you come to the party? ↔ I'll see you at the party.

❌ Common Mistakes

going back in Italy
✓ going back to Italy
Welcome in our country!
✓ Welcome to our country!
get to home
✓ get home (no preposition!)

🚌 IN vs ON for Transport

VehicleUseExample
car / taxiin"Laura arrived in a taxi."
bus / train / planeon"There were too many people on the bus."
ship / boaton"We travelled on the ship."
bike / motorbike / horseon"Jane passed me on her bike."
🚗

Memory Trick: Small & private = IN, Large & public = ON

You sit in a car like you're inside a box. You board a bus like stepping on a platform. If it's big and public, use ON.

🛤️ BY for Method of Travel

by by car / by bus / by train
by by plane / by boat / by bike
on on foot (NOT by foot)
⚠️ Use by only without an article: "by car" ✓ but "in my car" (with article, use IN).

🚪 INTO vs OUT OF

into go into a room (= enter)
into get into a car / climb into bed
into crash into a wall

Opposite: out of — "She got out of the car and went into a shop."

✈️ Arrive IN vs AT

UsePrepositionExample
Town or countryin"They arrived in Madrid."
Building or eventat"She arrived at the airport."
Home (no preposition!)—"I got home late." (no 'to')

IN, ON, AT — Other Important Uses

Go beyond time and place — learn the special phrases you need to sound fluent.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use by for method, means, and proximity
  • Use in in phrases like "in love", "in a mood", "in Russian"
  • Use on in phrases like "on fire", "on holiday", "on purpose"
  • Understand "at" with speed, age, and temperature

IN Fixed phrases with IN

in in the rain / in the sun / in the dark
in in Russian / in French (languages)
in in dollars / in euros (currencies)
in in love (with somebody)
in in a good/bad mood
in in my opinion
in in a hurry / in trouble

ON Fixed phrases with ON

on on business / on a diet
📺

Memory Trick: ON = Broadcasting/Active state

When something is ON, it's broadcasting or in an active state: on TV, on the radio, on fire, on strike — they're all "switched on".

BY Using BY for Method & More

UseExample
Method of doing"I paid by credit card." / "Send by post."
Accidental events"We met by chance." / "I took it by mistake."

Difference/margin"He won by five metres."

pay by cash
✓ pay cash / pay in cash
by the chance
✓ by chance (no article!)
by foot
✓ on foot
Module 5 · Verb + Preposition

Verb + Preposition Combinations

Many verbs require a specific preposition. These must be memorised!

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use listen to, look at, wait for correctly
  • Understand the difference between look for and look after
  • Use think about vs think of
  • Master verbs followed by TO vs AT
Verb + TO/AT
Verb + FOR/ABOUT
Common Pairs
Quiz

Verb + TO

to listen TO (music/the radio)
to talk/speak TO somebody
to apologise TO somebody
to explain something TO somebody
to describe something TO somebody

❌ NO "to" needed

phone to somebody
✓ phone somebody
ask to me
✓ ask me
answer to my email
✓ answer my email

Verb + AT

at look/stare/glance AT
at laugh AT somebody
at shout AT (when angry)
at aim/point/shoot AT
shout AT vs shout TO:
Shout at = aggressive ("He shouted at me.")
Shout to = to be heard ("She shouted to me from across the street.")

Verb + FOR

for wait FOR somebody/something
for look FOR (= search for)
for ask FOR something
for apply FOR a job
for search FOR something

Verb + ABOUT

about talk/read/know/think ABOUT
about worry/complain/dream ABOUT
about do something ABOUT a problem

❌ No preposition with DISCUSS

discuss about the problem
✓ discuss the problem

🔀 Easily Confused Pairs

PairMeaningExample
look FORsearch / try to find"I'm looking for my keys."
look AFTERtake care of"Who looks after the children?"
think OFproduce an idea"I thought of a great plan."
think ABOUTconsider carefully"I'm thinking about your offer."
hear OFknow that s.o. exists"I've never heard of him."
hear FROMreceive contact"Have you heard from Jane?"
care FORtake care of / like"Who cares for him?"
care ABOUTthink s.t. is important"He doesn't care about others."
Module 6 · Noun + Preposition

Noun + Preposition Combinations

Many nouns require a specific preposition. Learn the most important patterns here.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use cause of, reason for, solution to correctly
  • Distinguish increase/fall IN vs other noun patterns
  • Master relationship/connection patterns with FOR, TO, WITH, BETWEEN
  • Avoid the most common noun+preposition errors
OF / FOR
TO / IN / WITH
Quiz

Noun + OF

of the cause OF the explosion
of a picture / photo OF the family
of a map / drawing / plan OF the city
of the advantage OF living alone

Noun + FOR

for a demand FOR the product
for a need FOR change
for a reason FOR the delay
for an invitation FOR vs TO: "invitation TO the party"

❌ Common Mistakes

the reason of the delay
✓ the reason for the delay
no demand of the product
✓ no demand for the product

Noun + TO

to a solution TO the problem
to an answer TO a question
to a reply TO a letter
to a reaction TO something
to damage TO the car
to an invitation TO a party
to an attitude TO/TOWARDS work

❌ Common Mistakes

a solution of the problem
✓ a solution to the problem

Noun + IN / WITH / BETWEEN

in an increase / fall / rise IN prices
with a relationship / contact WITH somebody
between a connection / difference BETWEEN two things
📈

Memory Trick: Changes go IN

When a number goes up or down — increase, decrease, rise, fall — the area affected always uses IN: "an increase in crime", "a fall in prices".

Module 7 · Adjective + Preposition

Adjective + Preposition Combinations

These adjective patterns are essential for natural-sounding English. Many must simply be memorised!

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Use afraid of, good at, interested in correctly
  • Master OF vs AT/WITH for emotions: "pleased WITH", "surprised AT"
  • Distinguish "married TO" from "full OF" / "crowded WITH"
  • Build confidence using adjective + preposition naturally
OF / AT / IN
WITH / FOR / ON / TO
Emotions
Quiz

Adjective + OF

of afraid / scared / frightened / terrified OF
of fond / proud / ashamed / jealous / envious OF
of suspicious / critical / tolerant OF
of aware / conscious OF
of capable / incapable OF
of typical OF
of short OF (= not enough)
of full OF (but: crowded WITH)

Adjective + AT / IN

at good / bad / brilliant / hopeless AT
in interested IN
in experienced IN / involved IN

❌ Common Mistakes

I'm good in maths.
✓ I'm good at maths.
She's interested for music.
✓ She's interested in music.

Adjective + WITH / FOR / ON / TO

PatternExamples
pleased / happy / satisfied / disappointed WITH"Were you happy with your results?"
crowded WITH"The streets were crowded with tourists."
famous FOR"Florence is famous for its art."
responsible FOR"Who is responsible for this?"
keen ON"I'm not very keen on spicy food."
dependent ON"He's dependent on his parents."
similar TO"Your style is similar to mine."
married / engaged TO"She's married to a doctor."
different FROM / TO"This is different from what I expected."

❌ Common Mistakes

married with an American
✓ married to an American
different of what I expected
✓ different from what I expected

😤 Emotional Adjectives + Prepositions

EmotionPrepositionExample
surprised / shocked / amazed / astonishedAT or BY"I was surprised at/by the news."
pleased / satisfied / happy / delightedWITH"They were delighted with the present."
disappointedWITH"We were disappointed with the hotel."
sorryABOUT / FOR"I'm sorry about the mess." / "Sorry for shouting."
angry / annoyed / furiousABOUT / WITH"Are you annoyed with me for being late?"
excited / worried / nervous / happyABOUT"Are you nervous about the exam?"
bored / fed upWITH"I'm fed up with waiting."
tiredOF"I'm tired of waiting."
💡

Memory Trick: Result vs Process

BORED/FED UP WITH something you experience. TIRED OF something ongoing. Think: you're bored with a movie (experience), tired of the commute (repeated process).
